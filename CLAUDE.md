# The Red Frontier (NASA Space Apps 2026)

Game title: **The Red Frontier**. Unity 6 game for the Space Apps challenge "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer".
Design rule: every element must have a reason and be used in play (no decoration-only systems or props).
Deadline: submissions close Sunday Nov 15, 2026, 11:59 PM local. Goal: all features built Saturday, bug fixing Sunday.
Ships as a Unity WebGL build on itch.io (browser, no download, no account).

## Audience and tone
- Kids ages 7–12. Short sentences, icons, plain words. Every science word gets a tap-for-meaning note
  (tau = "how dusty the sky is"; sol = "one Mars day, about 40 minutes longer than ours").
- No player dies. Failure = "safe abort" + debrief that explains what happened. Never punish curiosity.
- Honest framing: badge every real figure as Real (Live or Archived + date). Player-facing text says only
  what is real; it never mentions practice, test or simulated data.

## Story
A solo astronaut (in a spacesuit) is invited to Mars at an unspecified future time
and runs a habitat at Meridiani Planum (Opportunity's landing site). Companion: HAB, the habitat computer.
Mission Control is a fictional agency: no NASA name or logo on characters or UI.
- Spine: the Red Frontier program will send the first crew of six. First, you prove a person can live at
  Meridiani Base. Each stage = one real rover event = one lesson added to the **Crew Handbook**
  (MissionLoader.Chapters holds stage, Mission Control message, lesson). Finale: the crew lands.
- Every element has a job: rover = real-data report (E); solar panels gather dust each sol, clean them on EVA (E);
  Radio/antenna = Mission Control messages arrive only with the Radio on (mission events "mc"); samples are
  studied by the Science lab (1 per sol) for science score; Freeplay credits buy upgrades in the Freeplay hub.
- Ease of use: title "Continue the expedition" goes straight to the next briefing; Cadet hints come with a
  waypoint marker; debrief's main button is the next step (next stage / try again).

## 3D, first person
- First-person 3D, realistic look. No body parts in view (primitive arms looked fake); the full suit is on
  the suit stand in the habitat (and later the debrief). Custom controller (CharacterController + Input System, Mars gravity).
- Art direction: realism first. Real Mars references (Meridiani soil is even reddish-tan with tiny sparse
  hematite spherules, soft ripples, pale layered bedrock; sun is small; sky butterscotch with a blue halo near the sun).
  Keep textures low-contrast and subtle; nothing cartoony or noisy.
- Habitat interior: walk to wall panels and press E to toggle systems; bed = next sol; airlock to the surface.
- Surface (Meridiani Planum, flat plain): sample sites, rover, radiation shelter. Outside = EVA (extra oxygen per sol).
- Hazards are visible and driven by each sol's data: tau -> dark sky + fog + dust; cold -> night + frost;
  radiation -> aurora + red warning lamp; softSand -> rover sinks.
- Meters, decision cards and debrief are UI overlays (PlaytestUI, worldMode on).
- Scene is generated by the menu Mission Trainer > Build Mars Scene (3D); it saves real material assets so
  WebGL keeps shaders. Rebuilding overwrites Mars.unity, so coordinate before hand-editing it.
- WebGL budget: URP, low-poly primitives/models, 1 sun + 1 habitat light, fog, one small particle system, no post FX.

## Core systems (only 3 meters)
- Power (battery %): solar input × sunlight × dust; drained by systems that are on.
- Oxygen (%): made by life support (needs power); used every sol and on EVAs.
- Shielding / radiation dose: shelter and shield upgrades reduce dose from solar events.
- Systems the player toggles: Life support, Heater, Radio, Greenhouse lights, Science lab (each costs power per sol).
- Food = small counter (greenhouse lights add, 1 used per sol). Comm delay: Earth replies arrive late;
  waiting costs a sol, a procedure card is immediate.
- RULE: no choice is free. Every decision card shows its cost before the player picks.

## Modes
- Story: Invitation → Training (tutorial) → Journey → 5 chapters:
  1. The Storm (Opportunity, June 2018 dust storm; power 100% → 74% → 28% → 5%; player chooses radio vs heater and goes silent)
  2. Cold Night (Spirit's loss in winter, 2010)
  3. Stuck (Spirit at Troy 2009; Opportunity at Purgatory Dune 2005)
  4. Silence (solar conjunction, ~2 weeks with no contact; procedure cards only)
  5. Solar Storm (Sept 2017 event: surface radiation doubled for ~2 days; reach shelter in time)
- Freeplay: endless generated missions (goal + 1–2 hazard cards), credits, upgrade shop, sol counter, high score.
  Only samples brought home count; healthy reserve = bonus. Solar events come from an embedded DONKI snapshot.
- Difficulty: Cadet (default, hints, big reserve, no timers), Pilot, Commander. Works in both modes.

## Suit and achievements
Cosmetic only (never changes gameplay). Applied in 3D as trim color, patch decals and visor material (AstronautSuit).
The suit color tints the whole suit (Classic white is the default); unlock suit colors, chapter patches,
gold sun visor (Ch5), purple visor (aurora), Commander red stripes (3 stars on all chapters), Freeplay sol patches.
Saved with PlayerPrefs.

## Scoring (Story)
Safety margin 40, Systems priority 25, Procedure 15, Shielding 10, Science 10 → 1–3 stars with breakdown.
Debrief: Your choices / What the real team did / What to try next time + one "Real science" card + sources.

## Data (cite all; show badges in game)
DONKI (space weather), RadLab (ISS dose), OSDR (astronaut health), Mars Trek (terrain/map),
NASA Open APIs (Mars Rover Photos, APOD), CSA food toolkit, Opportunity 2018 power data
(Jun 3 468 Wh, Jun 4 345 Wh tau 2.1, Jun 6 133 Wh tau >3, Jun 10 ~22 Wh tau 10.8).
Embed snapshots; optional live DONKI call with fallback. Never present static data as live.
"Science behind the game" page lists every dataset and why it is used.

## Engineering conventions
- One shared engine for Story and Freeplay. Missions are DATA (one file per chapter), not hard-coded scenes.
  Freeplay's generator outputs the same mission format.
- Keep scenes few; build UI from prefabs. Tell the team before editing a scene file (merge conflicts).
- Must run in WebGL: no threads, no file system writes, use UnityWebRequest, PlayerPrefs for saves.
- Small commits with clear messages; pull before starting work.
- Before big changes, propose a short plan first.
